The problem

Disputes arrive at the worst possible moment

A chargeback notice lands, the clock starts, and someone has to open the order, find the tracking number, work out whether the parcel moved, and write a reply that matches reality. Multiply that by every dispute and it becomes guesswork.

Slow, manual follow-up

Every dispute means digging through orders and fulfilments before a single word gets written to the customer.

The wrong message

Telling a customer their parcel is on the way when it was delivered — or the reverse — damages the conversation you were trying to save.

Duplicate emails

Webhooks retry, statuses update, someone re-syncs — and the customer receives the same message two or three times.

What you get

Organised, shipment-aware dispute communication

Everything Dispute Guard does is built around one idea: the message should match what actually happened to the order.

Automated dispute emails

The dispute reason and the shipment state together decide which email workflow runs, so the customer hears something relevant instead of a generic notice.

Shipment status detection

Unfulfilled, tracking added, in transit, out for delivery and delivered are each recognised from the order's fulfilment and tracking data.

Customisable templates

Edit the wording for every reason and shipment combination, preview it safely, send a test to yourself, and restore the default whenever you want.

Monitoring dashboard

Open disputes, cases waiting on manual review, email activity and revenue at risk are all visible from a single screen.

Manual review protection

Unknown, mixed, partial or inconsistent shipment data never triggers an automatic email. Those disputes are flagged and left to you.

No duplicate sending

A dispute can produce at most one initial automatic email. Repeated webhooks, status updates and manual re-syncs will not send it again.

How it works

From dispute notice to the right email

Five steps run automatically in the background once you have activated automation.

Detect the dispute

Dispute Guard listens for Shopify Payments dispute events and records each one against your store, ignoring anything it has already seen.

Match the order

The related order is fetched from Shopify so the reason, amount, customer and fulfilment history are read from the authoritative source rather than the notification.

Classify the shipment

Fulfilments, tracking numbers and carrier events are read together to place the order in one of five shipment states — or to mark it unknown.

Choose the template

The dispute reason and the shipment state select one of your templates. If the combination is disabled, or anything looks uncertain, the dispute is held for manual review instead.

Send once, and log it

One transactional email goes to the customer address held on the Shopify order, the result is written to your email log, and the dispute is marked so nothing is sent twice.

Dispute Guard does not submit evidence, accept disputes or contact banks. It handles the customer conversation around a dispute — organised, timely and matched to the shipment — and leaves the dispute response itself in your hands inside Shopify.

Shipment awareness

Five states it recognises — and one it refuses to guess

Shipment state is worked out conservatively. Clear signals produce a clear state; anything mixed, partial or contradictory falls back to review rather than an assumption about where a customer's parcel is.

Mixed states across multiple shipments, partially fulfilled orders, missing tracking and unsupported carrier events are all treated as unknown on purpose.

Questions

Frequently asked

Does Dispute Guard respond to the dispute for me?

No. It handles the customer-facing email around a dispute. Submitting evidence, accepting a dispute or dealing with the bank stays with you inside Shopify. Version 1 deliberately requests no write or evidence permissions at all.

Which payment providers are supported?

Shopify Payments disputes only. External gateways are not covered in this version.

Can it send more than one email per dispute?

No. Each dispute can produce at most one initial automatic email. Repeat webhook deliveries, dispute status updates and manual re-syncs never start a second send.

What happens if the shipment data is unclear?

The dispute is marked for manual review and no email goes out. That covers unknown states, unsupported reasons, mixed or partial fulfilment data, and missing customer or order information.

Can I change the wording of the emails?

Yes. Every reason and shipment combination has its own template you can edit, enable or disable, preview, test-send to your own address, and reset back to the default.

Can I try it before anything reaches a real customer?

Yes. Automation is off by default and test mode is on by default when the app is installed. You can send test emails to your own address using your real templates before activating anything.

What Shopify permissions does it need?

Read access to orders, customers and Shopify Payments disputes. Customer name and email require Shopify's protected customer data approval; without it, some disputes will fall to manual review rather than being sent automatically.
