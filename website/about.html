About

Built to say the right thing at the worst moment

Dispute Guard is a focused Shopify app for the part of a chargeback that merchants usually handle by hand: the message to the customer. It reads the order, reads the shipment, and sends the email that fits — or gets out of the way.

Our approach

A narrow tool that does one job carefully

Most dispute tooling tries to do everything: gather evidence, argue with the bank, predict outcomes. Dispute Guard does not. It takes the single task that is repetitive, time-sensitive and easy to get wrong — writing to the customer while the shipment is in a particular state — and makes it reliable.

That means detecting a dispute, fetching the authoritative order from Shopify, classifying where the shipment actually is, choosing the merchant's own template for that exact situation, and sending exactly one email. Everything else it treats as somebody else's job, on purpose.

The result is a dispute inbox that stays organised, customers who receive something accurate rather than generic, and a clear record of what was sent and when.

Principles

The rules the app follows

These are not marketing lines — they are the constraints the app is actually built around.

When in doubt, do nothing

Unknown shipment states, mixed or partial fulfilments, unsupported dispute reasons and missing customer data all stop automation and raise a manual review instead of producing a guess.

One email, never two

A dispute has a single initial automatic email. Duplicate webhook deliveries, later status updates and manual re-syncs are recorded but never trigger another send.

The store's data stays the store's

Each store's data is isolated from every other store. Access tokens are stored encrypted and never exposed to the browser, and sensitive content expires on a retention schedule.

The recipient comes from Shopify

Production emails always go to the customer address held on the real Shopify order. If that address has changed since the dispute was processed, the case goes to review rather than being redirected.

Nothing sends until you say so

Automation is off and test mode is on when the app is installed. You review templates, send a test to yourself, and explicitly activate automation before a customer ever receives anything.

Honest numbers

Revenue at risk counts only disputes that are genuinely open, kept separate per currency. Test emails never inflate your sending statistics.

In scope

What Dispute Guard does

  • Detects new Shopify Payments disputes for your store
  • Fetches the related order, customer and fulfilment data from Shopify
  • Classifies shipment state from fulfilments and tracking events
  • Chooses your template for that reason and state
  • Sends one transactional customer email when eligible
  • Logs every email, including tests, separately
  • Flags uncertain cases for manual review
  • Shows open disputes, reviews and revenue at risk
Out of scope

What it deliberately does not do

  • Submit dispute evidence to Shopify or the card network
  • Accept or decline a dispute on your behalf
  • Contact banks, card networks or acquirers
  • Handle disputes from payment gateways other than Shopify Payments
  • Send automatic follow-up sequences after the first email
  • Modify orders, refunds, fulfilments or any other store data
  • Guess a shipment state from incomplete information

Dispute Guard requests read-only Shopify permissions. It cannot change anything in your store.

Getting started

Onboarding takes one careful sitting

The app walks you through each step and will not switch automation on until they are done.

Confirm the connection

Install the app on your store and confirm that Dispute Guard can read your orders and Shopify Payments disputes.

Set your store details

Store name, support contact and reply-to address, so replies from customers reach the right inbox.

Review your templates

Read through the templates for each dispute reason and shipment state, and edit the wording to match your brand voice.

Send a test email

Send a real message to your own address using your real templates and the real mail path, with no Shopify dispute involved.

Activate automation

Turn off test mode and explicitly enable automation. Only then can a customer receive an automatic dispute email.
